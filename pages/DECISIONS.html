Plan vs. patent discrepancies (checked 2026-09-24 against US 11,598,261 B2 full text)

  • D1 — Intermediate pressure, LA. Summary gives ≈455 psia (col. 3 ll. 32-34); Fig. 3 gives ≈455 psia and ≈−230 °F (col. 14 ll. 27-29). Fig. 2 says “usually about 460 psia” (col. 9 ll. 54-55). Validation target: 455 psia (±10% covers both).
  • D2 — Intermediate pressure, LNG. ≈520 psia (Summary, col. 3 ll. 32-34; Fig. 1 “usually about 520 psia”, col. 6 ll. 59-60). Stated “assuming that 100% of the pumped compressed liquefied fluid is re-condensed”. Validation uses 100% re-condensing.
  • D3 — P-1 discharge range. Summary says 1000–2000 psia (col. 3 ll. 8-9); the figure descriptions say 1000–2500 psia (e.g. Fig. 1, col. 5 ll. 36-37). Use 1000–2500 psia as the slider/sweep range.
  • D4 — Mechanism. Patent says the intermediate pressure is the saturation pressure at the temperature the hot stream is cooled to, which depends on the E-1 approach temperature. The plan’s internal-pinch formulation is the general form of this (the pinch may be internal, not at the cold end). Keep the plan’s.
  • D5 — Fig. 1 pass-2 heating has no fired heater. Pass 2 (pump 126 → warming HX 128 → LT recuperator 122 → T-2 130) is heated only by the warming fluid and by T-1 exhaust in recuperator 122. All external heat enters pass 1 (GT exhaust HX 118 and 112, HT recuperator 114 via splitter 110 / mixer 116). The plan’s generic “H-2 re-heats” becomes optional per preset.
  • D6 — Fig. 1 P-2 discharge. Patent: 500–1500 psi above the receiving pipeline pressure. P-2 default is defined relative to pipeline pressure for LNG presets.
  • D7 — Fig. 3 ORC fluid. Patent: “preferably methane” (col. 15 ll. 49-50). Default ORC fluid = Methane. Fig. 3 ORC condensate ≈−130 °F / 530 psia (col. 16 ll. 36-38; extra reference point, sanity check only).
  • D8 — Fig. 4 claim. “including the ORC using a portion (about 25%-30%) of the exhaust stream, will produce about 1.5 times the power of the gas turbine generator 432” (col. 19 ll. 1-4). Exhaust split default 0.275, range 0.25–0.30. 1.5× is a sanity check only. The Fig. 4 ORC is “not shown” in the patent, so its configuration is our assumption.
  • D9 — Peak window. Patent background: evening peak “approximately 6 PM to 10 PM”. Default 18:00–22:00 confirmed.
  • D10 — Fig. 5 turbocharger air. ≈45 psia and ≈450 °F at the turbocharger discharge (col. 20 ll. 46-47).
  • D11 — No stream tables. The patent has no heat-and-material balances or performance tables. The only quantitative anchors are the pressures/temperatures/ranges above. VALIDATION.md is limited to them.

Project decisions (user-confirmed 2026-09-24)

  • D12 — Stack and serving. A regular server-rendered web app: Flask + Jinja + plain HTML/CSS + small vanilla JS modules, with no Node/npm, bundler or client-side framework (this replaces the plan’s original FastAPI + React/Vite). make dev = flask run --debug on 0.0.0.0:5001 (dev only, no auth). Pages work without JS; JS only adds live slider preview, charts, click-to-inspect and job polling.
  • D13 — Repo root is ~/jitmodel, with pyproject.toml, the jitmodel/ package and .venv/ at the root.
  • D14 — UI is built from §7 tokens and the Phase 9 description; there is no separate mockup. The flowsheet diagram is inline SVG rendered on the server (web/diagram.py) instead of a JS canvas library.
  • D15 — Validation defaults (patent does not state them): E-1 ΔT_min 5 °F (2.78 K), P-1 discharge 1500 psia, pump η_s 0.75, expander η_s 0.85, generator η 0.97, T-1 inlet per preset. Results are not tuned to hit targets.
  • D16 — Python toolchain. Python 3.13 (Debian apt) with python3 -m venv .venv + pip install -e '.[dev]', replacing the plan’s original Python 3.12 + uv.
  • D17 — Browser assets. Plotly.js and IBM Plex fonts are vendored under jitmodel/web/static/ (version, source URL and SHA-256 recorded here when added); no CDN at runtime. The E2E smoke test uses the Python playwright package with the system /usr/bin/chromium; playwright install is never run.
  • D18 — Git hooks. A plain .githooks/pre-commit script runs make lint (enabled with core.hooksPath); the pre-commit framework is not used.
  • D19 — Package vetting. Every Python package is checked on PyPI before it’s downloaded (name, source repo, release history, what requires it). Phase 0 audit (2026-09-25): all 35 installed packages verified. The three young ones are ast-serialize and librt (mypy deps, mypyc org) and typing-inspection (pydantic dep, pydantic org).

Phase 1 — properties layer (2026-09-25)

  • D20 — Methane temperature range. CoolProp’s Methane EOS (Setzmann & Wagner 1991) is validated 90.6941–625 K. CoolProp extrapolates above 625 K without error; the round-trip tests cover up to 700 K (PLAN.md range) and pass. States above t_max() are allowed but will be flagged in results (Phase 4+). The lower end is the triple point / melting line, not 90 K: at 20 MPa methane freezes at ~95.7 K. state_pt below the melting line raises PropertyError.
  • D21 — Pseudo-pure Air two-phase handling. CoolProp “Air” is pseudo-pure (Lemmon et al. 2000) with a bubble/dew glide from ancillaries (e.g. 106.2 / 108.1 K at 1 MPa), so PLAN.md §6’s “ignores the glide” is only partly true. CoolProp rejects P,T inside the glide, and its P,h flash near the bubble line disagrees with its own P,Q saturation (fails, or reports liquid at quality ≈0.007). Below Pc we determine the phase ourselves against the P,Q bubble/dew states; T, h and s are exactly linear in quality at fixed P in CoolProp’s pseudo-pure model, so quality is recovered exactly and the state is flashed with P,Q. Single-phase states get the phase imposed.
  • D22 — Near-critical band. Round-trip tests exclude |T−Tc| < 2 K with |P−Pc| < 5 % of Pc. At exactly P = Pc with T < Tc, CoolProp’s flash fails; the pressure is nudged up by 1e-6 relative and retried.
  • D23 — Caching and tables. Flash results are LRU-cached per fluid instance, keyed on inputs rounded to 12 significant digits (the flash uses the rounded inputs, so results are deterministic). The optional BICUBIC&HEOS backend writes its tables to var/coolprop-tables/ (git-ignored), never ~/.CoolProp.
  • D24 — Phase 1 benchmark (HEOS, 10k distinct state_ph calls, no cache, aarch64 container): Methane 0.39 s, Air 0.31 s (target < 1 s). A 20k-point random round-trip stress test per fluid: 0 failures, worst relative T error 9.3e-10.

Phase 2 — unit operations (2026-09-25)

  • D25 — Sign convention. heat_in and work_in are W into the fluid (pump work_in > 0, expander < 0); electric_power is W at the terminals (> 0 generated, < 0 consumed). Every unit satisfies Σ(m h)_in + heat_in + work_in = Σ(m h)_out; tests hold it to 1e-9 relative.
  • D26 — Drive efficiencies. Expanders take eta_gen (PLAN.md). Pumps take eta_motor, which the plan doesn’t mention; it defaults to 1.0, so pump electrical demand equals shaft power unless the user sets it.
  • D27 — Mixer outlet pressure is the lowest inlet pressure (higher-pressure inlets are throttled, isenthalpic).
  • D28 — Accumulator (V-1) passes the stream through and returns a warning, rather than raising, when the inlet is not saturated/subcooled liquid (quality > 1e-6). The flowsheet solver decides whether that’s a failure, because intermediate recycle iterates may be temporarily two-phase.
  • D29 — Splitter gives the last outlet the remainder of the mass flow, so mass closes exactly.
  • D30 — Heater has outlet-temperature and duty modes plus an optional pressure drop (default 0). Coupling to a heat-source stream comes in Phase 5.

Phase 3 — HX and intermediate pressure (2026-09-25)

  • D31 — E-1 hot outlet. The hot side is condensed exactly to saturated liquid (bubble point at P_int). That is the least duty that meets “fully condensed”, so it’s the most feasible choice. Subcooling only adds duty and tightens the cold end.
  • D32 — Pinch location. For both fluids at the patent conditions the pinch is internal, just above the hot dew point, where the supercritical cold stream’s cp peak meets the desuperheating vapor. So P_int doesn’t depend on the T-1 inlet temperature, T-1 efficiency, or recuperation ahead of E-1 (docs/VALIDATION.md).
  • D33 — Lowest-root search. The feasibility margin (min ΔT − ΔT_min) is scanned upward over 24 log-spaced pressures from the cold-end bound (condensing T = cold inlet T + ΔT_min) to 0.995·Pc, and the first sign change is refined with brentq. That guarantees the lowest feasible pressure even if the margin isn’t monotonic. About 30 E-1 evaluations and ~0.4-0.5 s per solve.
  • D34 — HX discretization. Equal-duty grid (N = 200) plus explicit nodes at both streams’ bubble/dew points (subcritical sides only), with bounded scalar refinement of the minimum in the two adjacent segments. P_int is identical to 3 decimals in psia for N = 50, 200 and 800. No pressure drop through exchangers in v1.
  • D35 — Analytic test fluids. tests/toy_fluids.py has a constant-cp gas and a toy condensable fluid (constant latent heat, Clausius–Clapeyron). They are used only for closed-form solver checks, never for results.

Phase 4 — flowsheet solver and Fig. 1 (2026-09-25)

  • D36 — Fig. 1 default inputs (the patent doesn’t state them):
    • m_dot 10 kg/s; pipeline 1000 psia; P-2 2000 psia (patent: 500–1500 psi above pipeline, col. 7 ll. 5-7).
    • Warming exchangers 108/128 to 75 °F (midpoint of the patent’s 50–100 °F, col. 5 ll. 58-59).
    • T-1 inlet 600 °F (inside methane’s validated range, D20; GT exhaust range is 400–2000 °F, col. 7 ll. 38-40).
    • Split to recuperator 114 is 0.5. All are inputs; none are tuned to a result.
  • D37 — P_int as a design spec. E-1 sets P_int, but P_int sits upstream of T-1, so it is an outer loop around the flowsheet, not a tear: solve_intermediate (with the flowsheet’s actual E-1 hot inlet T after the first pass) → rebuild with T-1 P_out = P_int → solve → repeat until P_int changes < 1e-7 relative (2 passes at defaults).
  • D38 — Baseline pump discharge is max(P-1, P-2). The plan says “same pressures”, and the baseline has one pump. Using P-1 alone (1500 psia against a 1000 psia pipeline) would understate the conventional cycle.
  • D39 — Fig. 1 topology. 112 and 118 are heaters with temperature specs (GT-exhaust coupling comes in Phase 5). Mixer 116 runs in the patent’s preferred equal-temperature mode (col. 6 ll. 44-48): 112 heats to 114’s cold outlet T. 114 and 122 run at max duty for ΔT_min. Pass 2 has no fired heater (D5).
  • D40 — Tear selection is a brute-force minimum feedback edge set over streams on cycles (Tarjan SCCs), smallest set first, preferring streams the caller supplied guesses for. Fig. 1 needs two tears: T-1 exhaust (120-114) and E-1 hot inlet (122-106).
  • D41 — Convergence. Bounded element-wise Wegstein (q ∈ [−5, 0]), tolerance 1e-9 on tear variables scaled by |value| (enthalpy scale floored at 1e5 J/kg); fallback Broyden (broyden1) with initial Jacobian −I so its first step is direct substitution. scipy’s default initial Jacobian stepped Fig. 1 into an infeasible E-1 state.
  • D42 — Max-duty exchangers with inlets ≤ ΔT_min apart return zero duty (with the approach warning) instead of raising, so bad intermediate iterates don’t abort a solve. This replaces the Phase 3 behavior.

Phase 5a — heat sources, Fig. 1 coupling, Fig. 4 (2026-09-25)

  • D43 — Ideal-gas mixtures (props/ideal_gas.py). Species ideal-gas h, s°, cp come from CoolProp’s alpha0 part (N2, O2, Ar, CO2, H2O), tabulated on a 1 K grid from 200 to 2500 K and linearly interpolated. The reference is sensible enthalpy 0 at 25 °C, consistent with LHV at 25 °C with H2O vapor. Checked against ideal-gas air tables (Δh 298→1000 K within 0.2 %). Water in exhaust is always vapor; streams below their water dew point are flagged, and condensation isn’t modeled.
  • D44 — Burning the cycle’s own air (user choice). At the burner inlet the real-gas air stream is re-expressed as an ideal-gas air mixture at the same T and P (ToIdealGas). The reference change is booked as heat_in under category “reference”, so the energy balance stays exact and it isn’t counted as heat input. The unit warns if the real-gas departure exceeds 2 kJ/kg.
  • D45 — Combustion. Fuel is pure methane at 25 °C, burned completely (CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O). LHV = 802.29 kJ/mol = 50.01 MJ/kg, computed from NIST-JANAF formation enthalpies (CH4 −74.87, CO2 −393.51, H2O(g) −241.826 kJ/mol). Exhaust composition is methane burned in dry air to a specified wet O2 fraction. Air humidity is ignored.
  • D46 — Heat-source defaults are generic inputs (user choice), not vendor data: exhaust temperature, flow and O2 fraction; molten-salt cp 1500 J/(kg K); coolant cp 4180 J/(kg K). No GT/engine ratings are assumed. Checks that need a rating (Fig. 4’s 1.5×) are computed only when the user supplies one.
  • D47 — Wegstein backtracking. If an accelerated step lands where a unit can’t be evaluated (e.g. a temperature-spec exchanger asked to cool), the step is halved back toward the last pass output, up to 8 times. The coupled Fig. 1 needed this for badly perturbed guesses.
  • D48 — Fig. 4 default inputs:
    • HP turbine 426: inlet 900 °F, outlet 250 psia (inside Fig. 2’s 100–400 psia range for the analogous turbine, col. 10 ll. 42-43).
    • Duct burner 428: to 900 °F (patent 700–1100 °F, col. 18 ll. 55-57).
    • LP turbine 430: to 15.2 psia (“slightly above atmospheric”).
    • GT exhaust: 20 kg/s at 1000 °F, 14 % O2.
    • ORC split: 0.275 (patent 25–30 %).
    • Optional expander 434: not modeled.
  • D49 — Fig. 4 ORC (user choice: methane, like Fig. 3). Pump → recuperator → exhaust heater → turbine → recuperator → condenser 420 → accumulator. Its high pressure (1500 psia) is from Fig. 3’s ORC range; its condensing pressure (530 psia) is Fig. 3’s stated ORC condensate state (D7). The ORC flow is the smaller of the exhaust-heater limit and the condenser-420 limit, both at ΔT_min. At defaults the condenser (the pass-2 air) limits it, which is reported as extra.orc_condenser_limited. The ORC design is redone for each P_int because P-2’s outlet depends on it.
  • D50 — Presets fix their tear sets (Flowsheet.solve(tears=...)) when a smaller automatic set would tear streams that are hard to guess. Fig. 4 tears the T-1 exhaust, the E-1 hot inlet and the two ORC-loop streams, whose guesses are exact from the ORC design.

Phase 5b — Fig. 3, Fig. 5 (2026-09-25)

  • D51 — Engines are boundary streams, not modeled machines: exhaust (flow = air + fuel burned, from the engine’s air flow and exhaust O2), coolant loops (constant-cp liquid), and, for Fig. 3, an air sink at the engine’s intake P and aftercooler T. Engine power and efficiency effects are out of scope. Fig. 3 defaults sized so the heat balance closes:
    • 8 kg/s engine air against 10 kg/s liquid air.
    • P-2 1500 psia and T-2 inlet 900 °F, so T-2’s exhaust at 45 psia (≈182 °F) is warmer than the 120 °F air the engine needs. With 2000 psia and 800 °F it would be colder, and 310 couldn’t work.
    • ORC-heater exhaust outlet 1100 °F (top of the patent range), leaving enough exhaust heat for 324.
    • Exchanger 322 heats to within 20 °F of the HT coolant (col. 14 l. 66 - col. 15 l. 1). 320 runs at max duty.
  • D52 — Shared ORC design (cases/orc.py), used by Figs. 3 and 4. The flow is the smaller of the exhaust limit (heater pinch, or a set exhaust outlet temperature) and the condenser limit. Fig. 3’s ORC includes warming exchanger 356 after its pump.
  • D53 — Fig. 5 specs and defaults:
    • 518 heats pass 1 to T-1 520’s inlet; 536 heats pass 2 to T-2 538’s inlet.
    • 512 runs at max duty, capped at the T-1 target minus ΔT_min. Without the cap it overheats pass 1 whenever the exhaust is abundant, so 518 would have to cool.
    • 530 heats pass 2 to the aftercooler T minus ΔT_min, so 532 can return the turbocharger air at the aftercooler temperature as the patent requires (col. 20 ll. 48-53).
    • Recuperators 510/514/516/522 run at max duty.
    • T-1 inlet 600 °F: below the patent’s 700–1100 °F, to stay inside methane’s validated range (D20).
    • T-2 inlet 500 °F (“medium-temperature” exchanger 536).
    • Duct burner 542 defaults to 1200 °F: without a burner the case is infeasible, and at 1100 °F exchanger 536 still crossed.
    • Burner 540 and exchanger 534 are off by default. The patent says they depend on economics, and that 534 may add nothing without 540.
  • D54 — Temperature crosses fail the case. A converged flowsheet in which any exchanger with non-zero duty has a negative approach violates the second law. The result is status “failed” with the exchangers named, and streams and units are kept for diagnosis. Approaches below ΔT_min but still positive are warnings.
  • D55 — Exchangers and warming heaters never run backwards. Warming heaters (ambient) are heat-only. Temperature-spec exchangers whose inlet already meets the spec transfer nothing and warn. A zero-duty exchanger’s inlet difference isn’t treated as an approach. Before this, bad iterates could make a warming exchanger cool a stream, and a spec exchanger fail outright.
  • D56 — make format order. ruff format now runs before ruff check --fix. Previously a lint error stopped make before the formatter ran, so long lines needed manual wrapping.

Phase 5c — Fig. 2, Fig. 4A (2026-09-25)

  • D57 — Fig. 2 defaults and structure:
    • T-1 inlet 500 °F (210 target). Expander 224: 1000 °F / 250 psia. Combustor 232: 2000 °F (patent 1600–2400 °F).
    • Duct burner 238: 1300 °F. At 1200 °F exchanger 210 crossed.
    • Expander 230 split 0 (optional in the patent). At 20 % the default burner is too cold, and the case fails with a clear heat-shortage message.
    • The Rankine loop 250 uses the shared ORC design (with warming exchanger 258). Startup/fill valve 264 and mixer 260 are not modeled.
    • Exchangers 210, 222 and 236 are heated by the cycle’s own combustion products, so they carry no heat-source category: fuel is counted once, at 232 and 238.
  • D58 — Fig. 4A. The patent’s 4A drops the external gas turbine 432, whose exhaust heats Fig. 4’s ORC, so the ORC and condenser 420 are omitted. Pass 2 goes P-2 → warming 422 → recuperator 414. Combustor 428 to 2000 °F; duct burner 436 to 1100 °F; 410 (now an exhaust exchanger) runs at max duty down to the products’ water dew point.
  • D59 — Exhaust exchangers don’t condense. Max-duty exchangers can stop the hot side at its water dew point (hot_above_dew_point). Used for 410 in Figs. 4 and 4A. Fig. 4 is unchanged; Fig. 4A’s exit sits at its 109 °F dew point instead of an unmodeled 80 °F.
  • D60 — Heat shortages are reported. A temperature-spec exchanger that would need more duty than its hot side can give (cooled to the cold inlet temperature) fails with the required and available MW, instead of a property-range error.
  • D61 — Preset registry (jitmodel.cases.PRESETS): name → inputs model and solver, for the studies and web layers.

Phase 6 — studies (2026-09-25)

  • D62 — Optimizer. SLSQP in variables scaled to [0, 1], forward-difference gradients with a 1e-3 step (well above the ~1e-7 solver noise; locates optima to a few 1e-4 of the box), maxiter 30. Starts are the box center plus a seeded Latin-hypercube sample in [0.1, 0.9], run in parallel processes. The objective is scaled by its value at the start. A failed solve returns a penalty (+10 in scaled units, where feasible values are about −1), which pushes SLSQP back into the feasible region; tested against an evaluator that is infeasible past a boundary.
  • D63 — Sweeps run in a process pool (CoolProp state objects are not thread-safe) and return one row per point (inputs + scalar metrics). Failed points keep their row with status “failed” and None metrics, so gaps are visible. Tidy rows are plain records, not pandas (PLAN.md §2 reserves pandas for dispatch). Any float/int input of a preset can be swept or optimized, except n_segments.
  • D64 — Smoothness criterion for the Phase 6 acceptance sweep: monotone, |Δ²y| < 0.5 × the local |Δy| at every point, and one sign of curvature. A criterion against the global minimum slope wrongly flags ordinary concavity.

Phase 7 — dispatch (2026-09-25)

  • D65 — Dispatch model:
    • Time steps: hourly. Discharge up to the solved cycle’s cryogen flow, capped by the send-out limit. Charging up to liquefaction power ÷ liquefaction energy per kg. Both are user inputs with no defaults, as the plan says.
    • Part rate: net power and fuel scale linearly with discharge flow. Real plants have part-load curves; this is an approximation.
    • LP: tank level as a variable with a sparse balance row per hour, and a final inventory ≥ the initial one so the LP can’t profit by draining the tank. Charging and discharging in the same hour is allowed; it pays only if the cycle’s output per kg exceeds the liquefaction energy (possible with fuel or waste heat).
    • Revenue is electricity only. For LNG the stored gas has its own commodity value that isn’t counted, which understates LNG cases whose purpose is combined gas and power storage.
    • Energy ratio is liquefaction energy ÷ energy produced, as the patent defines it (col. 19 ll. 6-8).
  • D66 — Worker processes use a forkserver. Forking a multi-threaded process (numpy, HiGHS, and later the web server) can deadlock the children; Python 3.13 warns about it.
  • D67 — Money units: prices in $/MWh; fuel price in /GJLHVinternally(/MMBtu at the UI boundary through units.py, 1 MMBtu = 1.05505585262 GJ). Heat rate is J/J internally, Btu/kWh for display.
  • D68 — Test price data is synthetic (tests/fixtures/prices_synthetic_8760.csv, fixed seed: seasonal curve, evening peak, noise, rare spikes). It is labeled as such in its header and is never presented as market data. The price CSV loader accepts a column named “price” (any case) or the last numeric column, and skips ‘#’ comment lines.

Phase 8 — web routes, jobs, JSON (2026-09-25)

  • D69 — Display units. units.QUANTITIES is the single table of display units.
    • US customary is the default, since the patent works in psia and °F: psia, °F, lb/h, Btu/lb, $/MMBtu, Btu/kWh.
    • SI uses kPa, °C, K for temperature differences, kg/s, kJ/kg, $/GJ and kJ/kWh.
    • MW, kWh/t, $/MWh and t are shared.
    • Every numeric input of every preset maps to a quantity kind by explicit override or name pattern, and a test fails if any input has none.
    • The choice is set by ?units= and remembered in a cookie.
  • D70 — Form values show 6 significant digits (10342.1 kPa, not 10342.13594), and each field carries a hidden “exact” copy (exact.<name> = shown text | SI value). A field submitted unchanged uses the exact SI value, so defaults round-trip bit-for-bit in either unit system; an edited field uses what was typed. Blank optional fields become None; blank required fields take the model default. (Phase 9 change: Phase 8 showed 10 digits.)
  • D71 — Jobs. Every solve, sweep, optimization and dispatch is a job:
    • Orchestration runs in a small thread pool.
    • Preset solves run in one shared forkserver process pool (D66).
    • Sweeps and optimizations start their own pools.
    • Results are saved atomically as var/results/<id>.json (git-ignored), so they survive dev-server reloads.
    • Running and failed jobs live only in memory; a reload forgets running jobs, never finished ones.
    • “Solving…” pages refresh themselves (<meta refresh>) for the no-JS path.
  • D72 — /api/intermediate runs only the E-1 solve (~0.5 s) for live slider feedback. Cases whose T-1 inlet comes from a recuperator use T_warm + 150 K there, which is fine because P_int doesn’t depend on T-1 inlet temperature (D32). The full solve (Solve cycle) uses the real flowsheet.
  • D73 — Exports. JSON is the stored document (inputs + result, SI). CSV is a table in the current display units with units in the headers: streams for a solve, rows for a sweep, starts for an optimization, the hourly schedule for a dispatch.
  • D74 — Result schema. CycleResult.hx_profiles now carries the T–Q profile of every two-stream exchanger that transfers heat (for the Phase 9 heat-exchanger view), not just E-1’s.

  • D84 — Cloud Run: one instance, one process (user request, 2026-09-25).

    • The container runs gunicorn ([deploy] extra, vetted on PyPI) with one worker process and 8 threads, because running jobs and their status live in that process’s memory. Cloud Run is limited to max-instances 1 for the same reason: a second instance wouldn’t see the first one’s jobs or results.
    • CPU stays allocated between requests (--no-cpu-throttling), so sweeps and optimizations keep running after the request that started them returns.
    • With min-instances 0 the service scales to zero when idle. Saved results (var/results, held in memory on Cloud Run) are then lost; exports and Save case keep them.
    • JITMODEL_WORKERS sets the solver process-pool size, because a container can report the host’s CPU count rather than its own limit.
    • Runtime packages are pinned in requirements-deploy.txt, generated from the dev .venv.
    • gunicorn 26’s control socket is disabled; otherwise it writes to ~/.gunicorn.

  • D85 — Diagram lines never run behind boxes (inventor’s review, 2026-09-26: “a mixer that doesn’t mix and a splitter that doesn’t split”).

    • The model was right: in Fig. 1, S-1 (110) splits the flow 50/50 to E-2 and E-3 and M-1 (116) rejoins them. The drawing hid it: E-2 and E-3 sat side by side, so the S-1 → E-3 and E-2 → M-1 lines ran straight behind the other exchanger, and the flow looked like a single series line. Every figure had lines hidden this way.
    • Fig. 1 now draws E-2 above E-3, both between S-1 and M-1.
    • Any line whose direct route would pass behind a box now detours through the free gaps between rows and columns.
    • Lines that share a side of a box get their own connection points. On the left or right, the line that continues straight along the row keeps the middle and the others sit above or below it, toward their other end; on the top or bottom they spread sideways. Before this, every line met a box at the middle of its side, so unrelated streams looked joined (in Fig. 1, the T-1 exhaust and the pass-2 methane both entered E-5 at one point).
    • Arrowheads are half their former size (7 px), so junctions and corners stay visible.
    • Tests check every figure, including Fig. 5’s optional units: no line runs behind a box, and no two lines meet a box at the same point.
